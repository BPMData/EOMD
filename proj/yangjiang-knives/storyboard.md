# Storyboard: Yangjiang knives

Stage: storyboard · DRAFT · Concept: **A, The Mark, with C's title belt as a running gag** (Bryan, Nov 2, 2026:
"let's try A with the moving belt idea")

**How to read this table.** It's a scrolling page, so the rows are chapters, not seconds, and "Time" becomes
"Scroll" (roughly how many screens tall each one is). It's silent, so the third column is the **Interaction**: what
the reader can touch.

**Length:** about 40–50 phone screens; a 12–18 minute read.

**Signature idea:** each chapter opens with a mark struck into steel. Scrolling is the hammer: the die drops, the
metal dents, the mark holds, then the story unfolds beside it. Rows 0, 3, 4, 6, 7 and 9 carry it at full size;
row 11 hands it to the reader.

**The belt:** a small drawn championship belt is pinned in the bottom corner. Its plate shows who holds "best knife
country" in the chapter on screen. When the title changes hands, the belt slides across to the new holder (rows 3,
4, 6, 10). Tapping it opens the lineage so far. It stays small and never covers text.

**Specimen plates:** the knife lesson is split into short interactive plates, each placed where the history first
needs it, so it never reads as homework. Each plate looks like a page from a big illustrated reference book:
off-white paper, line art with flat fills, numbered callouts.

**Facts:** every number and name below comes from `sources/` (file in brackets). Lines in quotes are **DRAFT** copy,
for Bryan to change.

## Sound concept
- **What sound does here:** nothing. The page is silent (brief §3, §11: "everyone hates music on web pages"). The
  strikes are felt through motion: a short drop, a hard stop, a small recoil in the metal.
- **For the client:** no music and no sound effects. The weight of each stamp comes from how it moves.
