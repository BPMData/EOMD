# Concepts: Yangjiang knives

Stage: concepts · Nov 2, 2026 · From the approved brief and `research-notes.md` only.

**The page in one sentence:** the "best knife country" title is a mark stamped on steel, won and lost through laws
and lies, so judge the knife, not the stamp.

**Clichés to avoid:**
- dragons
- fake brush lettering
- gongs
- samurai
- katana-slash wipes
- sparks-and-anvil hero shots
- "ancient secrets"
- glowing Damascus close-ups
- a chef's hand doing a rock-chop

---

## A. The Mark (recommended)
**The idea:** every chapter turns on a mark stamped into steel. The reader scrolls, and the next mark strikes.

**What they'd see:**
- **Opening:** a blank steel strip fills the screen. Scrolling brings a die down: 阳江. Then the hook: "Yangjiang
  makes most of China's knives. Under the Qing, shipping one abroad was a crime." (DRAFT copy)
- **The marks, in order** (each the hero of its chapter, the stamp in the metal and the story beside it):
  - the Qing ban
  - SOLINGEN
  - MADE IN GERMANY (the warning label that backfired)
  - NIPPON → JAPAN → OCCUPIED JAPAN
  - 関 (the machine-made army sword)
  - 十八子 (Shibazi)
  - the fake-Japanese brand names
- **The primer** sits in the middle as a specimen plate, like the big illustrated reference books:
  - one chef's knife drawn as a labeled cutaway
  - sliders for edge angle and thickness behind the edge, with the cross-section reshaping and the edge-life bar
    moving
  - a single/double bevel toggle
  - the Wenwudao: one blade that's thin in front and thick at the heel
  - the garlic-smash cleaver: hardness vs toughness
- **Ending:** "What's stamped on yours?" A decoder for real label phrases: "Solingen" (protected by law) vs "German
  steel" (means nothing) vs "Japanese steel core" (where the steel came from, not the knife).

**Look:** a reference book's specimen plates crossed with an NYT scroll story.
- Off-white plate pages; knives drawn in precise line art with flat fills and numbered callouts.
- A serif for reading, a heavy condensed sans for the stamps.
- Each chapter gets its own punchy color field: China in red and gold, used the way Chinese packaging uses them;
  Solingen in iron blue; Japan in indigo.

**How it feels:** knowing, punchy, collectible. Steers away from: dark "ancient craft" moodiness, and soft UI cards.

**Sound:** silent, as agreed.

**Why recommended:** the marks are what the research kept turning up, they're true to this subject alone, and the
ending turns the history into something the reader does in their own kitchen.

## B. The Cross-Section
**The idea:** the whole page is one giant cut through a laminated blade, and scrolling travels down through its
layers. The blade itself is the timeline.

**What they'd see:**
- **Opening:** a knife seen end-on. Zooming in, the cross-section becomes the page.
- **The layers are eras:** the soft iron cladding is "the long ban", and the hard core is "the boom". Yangjiang's
  *xiagang* sandwich and Japan's *san-mai* turn out to be the same drawing.
- The primer is native here: every grind, bevel and angle is shown as a cross-section at its true shape.
- **Ending:** the reader's view rises back out of the steel to a whole knife, with the edge line glinting.

**Look:** blueprint and engineering drawing: hairline rules, dimension arrows, hatching for each metal.

**How it feels:** precise, nerdy, calm. Steers away from: decoration. **Risk:** the coolest-headed option. The jokes
have to work harder against a technical look.

**Sound:** silent.

## C. The Title Belt
**The idea:** "best knife country" is a heavyweight belt, and the page is its lineage.

**What they'd see:**
- **Opening:** a championship belt with an engraved plate: UNDISPUTED KNIFE CHAMPION OF THE WORLD.
- **The lineage, scrolled like a fight card:**
  - Sheffield holds the belt.
  - Solingen takes it on price ("Made in Germany", the warning label that backfired).
  - Japan goes from "cheap and flimsy" to contender.
  - China? "Banned from the federation since 1646" (the Qing code). Then the comeback tour.
- Each "fighter" gets a tale-of-the-tape card: steel, hardness, edge angle, weight. That's the primer, smuggled in
  as stats.
- **Ending:** "the judges' scorecard": the measurable things that actually decide a knife, and no country on it.

**Look:** comic panels and fight-poster type: big condensed numerals and halftone dots, all drawn, no photos.

**How it feels:** loud, funny, competitive. Steers away from: reverence. **Risk:** the gimmick can wear thin over a
long page and tip into "cheesy", and the forced-labor facts sit awkwardly inside a fight poster.

**Sound:** silent.
