# Deep research prompt: what makes a good chef knife

Paste everything below the line into a deep-research tool (Claude Research, or similar). Bring back the full report
with its sources.

---

I'm writing a long, interactive web infographic for curious adults ("knife nerds") about knife-making in China
(especially Yangjiang), how Germany and Japan became known as the countries for good knives, and how China is
competing now. Readers need a primer on chef knives first, or the history won't make sense. Research that primer.

**Rules for the report**
- Every factual claim gets a source link right next to it. Prefer primary or expert sources: metallurgy papers and
  textbooks, steel makers' data sheets (Böhler, Uddeholm, Sandvik/Alleima, Takefu, Hitachi/Proterial, Aichi, Daido,
  Chinese mills), knife makers' published specs, controlled tests (CATRA edge-retention tests, toughness tests such
  as Knife Steel Nerds' Charpy data), and trade or government data. Mark forum posts, retailer copy and marketing as
  weak sources.
- Where experts disagree or evidence is thin, say so. Separate measured facts from craft lore.
- Give numbers with units and ranges (degrees, mm, grams, HRC), not adjectives.
- Write for a smart non-expert; define each term once.

**1. Anatomy and shape**
- Parts of a chef knife (tip, belly, heel, spine, choil, bolster, tang, handle) and what each does.
- The main kitchen shapes and how they're used: German/Western chef's knife, gyuto, santoku, nakiri, usuba, deba,
  yanagiba, bunka, kiritsuke, and the Chinese cleaver/caidao (slicer vs chopper vs bone cleaver). How the blade
  profile (flat vs curved belly) changes the cutting motion (rocking vs push-cutting vs chopping).

**2. The edge and grind**
- Single bevel vs double bevel: how each cuts, who uses which, why single bevels are traditional in Japan.
- Edge angles: typical German vs Japanese vs Chinese factory angles (per side and inclusive), and the trade-off
  between sharpness and edge stability.
- Grind types (flat, full convex, hollow, sabi/kataba, scandi, S-grind) and "thinness behind the edge": why it often
  matters more than the steel for how a knife feels when cutting.
- Spine thickness and distal taper: typical ranges and what they change.

**3. Size and balance**
- Typical lengths and weights for each shape; how weight and balance point affect control and fatigue.
- Full vs partial vs hidden tang; Western vs Japanese (wa) handles.

**4. Steel**
- What hardness (HRC) means, and the trade-off between hardness, toughness, edge retention and corrosion resistance.
- Carbon vs stainless vs powder-metallurgy steels. For each of these, give composition, typical hardness in kitchen
  knives, and strengths/weaknesses: X50CrMoV15 (1.4116), 5Cr15MoV, 3Cr13/4Cr13, 7Cr17MoV, 8Cr13MoV, 9Cr18MoV,
  AUS-8, AUS-10, VG-10, 10Cr15CoMoV, Sandvik 14C28N, Aogami (Blue) 1/2/Super, Shirogami (White) 1/2, SG2/R2, ZDP-189,
  HAP40, M390, Damascus/san-mai cladding (and whether the pattern adds performance).
- Which Chinese steels are copies or equivalents of Japanese or European ones, and who makes them.
- How much heat treatment matters compared to steel choice. Evidence that the same steel performs very differently
  between makers.

**5. Does the country matter?**
- What actually separates a great knife from a mediocre one in measurable terms (geometry, heat treatment, grind
  consistency, fit and finish, QC), and how much of that is tied to a place vs to a single maker.
- OEM/white-label manufacturing: which well-known Western or Japanese-branded knives are documented as made in China
  (especially Yangjiang), and what the evidence is. Country-of-origin labeling rules (US, EU, Japan) and loopholes.
- Any blind tests or controlled comparisons of knives across countries and price points.

**6. Yangjiang today (if found along the way)**
- Production and export figures for Yangjiang and China's cutlery industry, with dates and sources (Chinese
  government or industry association data preferred).
- Supply chain: steel mills, forging, grinding, heat treatment, handles, packaging: how much sits in one region.
- Technology: automated grinding, laser cutting, robotic sharpening, in-house powder steels.

**End with:** a table of the 10 most important design choices in a chef knife, each with: what it is, the usual
options, the measurable trade-off, and one source.
