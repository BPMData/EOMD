# Yangjiang and China's Knife Industry (1990–2026): Scale, Technology, and the Fight for Prestige

China already dominates world knife trade by volume and value, with 51.5% of world HS 8211 knife exports in 2024 (ranked #1 of 190 exporters, per OEC), and Yangjiang makes most of China's output. Japan and Germany still keep the premium price tier, brand prestige and steel provenance, although Chinese direct-to-consumer brands are starting to close the gap at the mid-price level.

## TL;DR
- **Scale:** In 2024 China shipped 51.5% of world knife exports by value (HS 8211, $1.66B), against Germany's $292M and Japan's $167M. Yangjiang says it makes about 75% of China's knives and scissors and handles about 85% of China's exports.
- **Capability:** Yangjiang has moved up the value chain. It now has in-house steelmaking, domestic powder-metallurgy and Damascus steels, robotic grinding, laser cutting and vacuum heat treatment. Its best direct-to-consumer brands (Xinzuo/Hezhen, Shibazi) get credible praise from reviewers, but mostly as "value" picks, not prestige ones.
- **Limits:** Prestige still sits with Solingen and Seki. Fake "Japanese" branding, uneven heat treatment and a US tariff stack of roughly MFN + 7.5% + 12.5% on Chinese kitchen knives (as of Oct 2026) hold back the brand shift.\[1\] US-bound knife exports already appear to be falling.

## Trend Summary
Since the 1990s, when private firms spun out of state-run cutlery works such as Yangjiang's Second Light Industry knife factory, Yangjiang has grown from anonymous OEM export into the world's volume centre for blades.\[15\] It now pursues automation, domestic premium steels and its own brands. China's global share is already about 50%, so further gains will have to come from value per knife, not volume. Here the evidence is mixed. Reviewers now recognise a small group of brands that label themselves honestly as Chinese, but price premiums and prestige still belong to Solingen and Seki, and US tariffs are cutting into China's largest market.

## Details

**Vertical integration**
- **Steel:** Shibazi Group (十八子) runs its own specialty steel plant, Shibazi Precision Special Steel. Its plant manager said imported steels used to cost ¥30,000–40,000+/t, while self-developed grades cost ¥18,000–19,000/t (Yangjiang TV via Sohu, 2021).\[16\] This is a company claim, so confidence is medium. The Yangjiang Alloy Materials Laboratory, opened Oct 2019 and led by Prof. Shang Chengjia of USTB, reports work on powder steel, high-carbon and high-nitrogen martensitic stainless (including Shibazi's nitrogen-alloyed "三好钢"). It is planning a "high-end powder-steel preparation-and-rolling" demonstration line. Its patents are used by 20+ local firms.\[17\]\[18\]\[19\]\[20\]
- **Powder metallurgy and Damascus:** Tuobituo (拓必拓) and Wuhan University of Technology report a "domestic powder-metallurgy Damascus martensitic stainless steel" and mass production of an M390-class powder steel "on par with foreign standards" (Shenzhen News, 2023).\[21\] This is a university/company claim with no independent test data, so confidence is low–medium. 14Cr14MoVNb is sold by Xinzuo as a powder steel at "62–64 HRC" under "72/73-layer" Damascus cladding.\[22\] **Hardness and layer counts are seller marketing claims.** Buyers on BladeForums and Russian forums say the composition looks like S35VN and question the 64 HRC figure.\[23\]\[24\]
- **Upstream and support industries:** Local specialist suppliers cover stamping, heat treatment, machinery, handles (for example, Kena coloured-wood handles) and packaging.\[25\] The fair organisers claim a "one-hour supply-chain circle" that can go from ore to finished knife in 15 days.\[26\] Treat this as promotional. At the Oct 2026 fair, high-end steel and knife exhibitors included Germany's 萨普, Switzerland's 合瑞迈 and Dumasteel, France's 埃赫曼 (Eramet), and Japan's T-AREA and H&K (China News Service), which shows that imported premium steel is still in demand.
- **OEM/ODM:** Zwilling says Henckels-branded (lower-tier) lines are made in Spain and China.\[27\] Retailers and reviewers report that Dalstrong and imarku manufacture in Yangjiang.\[28\] Xinzuo's Australian distributor says Henckels, Victorinox, Kershaw, Spyderco and Cuisinart produce lines in Yangjiang and that "only ~5%" of Yangjiang makers sell under their own name.\[29\] This is an interested-seller claim; confidence is low.

**Technology**
- Jinhui (金辉, owner of the Wangmazi brand) runs automatic stamping that turns out 120,000 pieces/day, which it says equals 24 skilled workers working 10 hours. It also uses robot-arm wet grinding with one operator per three arms. Meilong Meili (美珑美利) uses vacuum heat treatment, laser cutting and laser welding.\[30\] Zhangxiaoquan's Yangjiang base automates blanking, wet grinding and edging, using machine vision and constant-force control. Suzilan (苏仕兰) reports an ¥80M line with an "isothermal explosive quench" that takes blades from 1,400°C to 200°C in 5 seconds (company claim).\[31\]\[32\]
- The association president describes two stages: first automation, then "intelligent" lines with numerical-control compensation and vision sensing. Wangmazi is targeting a "lights-out" plant.\[11\]\[15\]
- **Domestic steel vs Japanese steel:** 10Cr15CoMoV is close to VG-10 in composition (≈1% C, 15% Cr, 1.5% Co, plus Mo and V).\[33\] Both are typically hardened to about 58–62 HRC.\[34\]\[35\] Reviewer ChefPanko found that well-heat-treated samples match VG-10 in cutting, but poorly treated ones rusted where VG-10 did not.\[36\] **The steel name says less than the heat treatment.** No independent lab data comparing 14Cr14MoVNb with SG2 (R2) was found, so "Chinese SG2" claims are unverified.

**Brand shift**
- Xinzuo and its sister brand Hezhen sell openly as "Made in China" through Amazon and AliExpress.\[33\] ChefPanko, after testing 100+ Chinese knives, called Xinzuo potentially "the number one kitchen knife manufacturer in China… in terms of quality control, materials, and heat treatment." He also said its 10Cr15CoMoV outperformed other brands using the same steel.\[37\]\[38\] Community figures still place the best Chinese brands (Xinzuo, Hezhen, Shibazi, Dengjia) as good value, not as equals of Japanese handmade knives.\[33\]
- In a 2016 review, Shibazi found that local brands controlled about 90% of developed-market kitchen-knife sales. It now exports to 20+ countries, with knives that sell for $10–20.\[39\] Zhangxiaoquan (Hangzhou) and Wangmazi (Beijing, bought by Jinhui) have moved or expanded production in Yangjiang.\[32\]\[40\]\[41\] "Yangjiang Hardware Knives & Scissors" was named one of MIIT's first "China Consumer Name Brands" (regional) in 2025.\[5\]
- Kickstarter-funded Chinese knife brands exist, but no reliable campaign figures were verified for this report.

## Caveats and Counterpoints

1. **Self-reported shares.** The 70–75% and 85% figures come from Yangjiang's own government and fair organisers.\[32\]\[42\] They have barely moved in two decades: the 2002 baseline was 60% of national output and 80% of exports (21st Century Business Herald), the mayor quoted 70%/85% in 2016, and China News Service (Oct 2026) still gives 75%/85%, saying export scale grew from ¥1.7B to over ¥8B over the fair's 25 years. None of these figures is published with a method. *Confidence: medium.*
2. **The headline number is inflated.** The ">¥55B" figure covers the whole hardware chain and has been unchanged since 2022. Above-size knife producers make only ¥7.89B.\[5\]\[39\] Use the ¥3.39B customs export figure for knife exports. *High.*
3. **An unverified figure is in circulation.** A Tencent self-media post and Baidu Baike cite full-year 2025 Yangjiang knife exports of ¥41.3B... more precisely ¥4.13B (41.3亿元).\[43\]\[44\] This is plausible, given Jan–Sep 2025 was about ¥3.14B, but no official release was found. *Low.*
4. **Share is not prestige.** Germany's and Japan's knife exports are much smaller in value but command higher prices and protected origin names. Seki's knives and Solingen's (Wüsthof: 112 robots supporting 350+ staff at three Solingen sites, about 1.7M forged knives a year, with hand-finished edges) are still the reference points. The weakness of incumbents is real but limited: Zwilling's kitchen division had €855M in 2025 revenue (Werhahn Group Annual Report 2025), and the company is restructuring, with job cuts and the closure of a Solingen logistics site (Solinger Tageblatt). *Medium.*
5. **"Fake Japanese" branding.** Many Amazon and dropship brands (for example Huusk, Kawahiro, Seido, Mitsumoto Sakari) use Japanese-sounding names for Yangjiang-made knives.\[28\]\[45\]\[46\] Kawahiro's registered owner is a shell company named "Japan WIN Forged Knife Co."\[47\] This undermines trust in honestly labelled Chinese brands. *Medium–high.*
6. **Quality control.** Zhangxiaoquan's 2022 "garlic-smashing" scandal involved a ¥99 50Cr15MoV "Longque" (龙雀) cleaver that snapped. In its 15 Jul 2022 statement, the company said the model had sold 60,000+ units since 2018 and drawn 11 formal complaints (0.0183%). The episode, a repeat break reported by a Xuzhou consumer on 20 Feb 2023, and later e-commerce review manipulation tied to the fallout hurt a 400-year-old brand. Hard, thin Chinese blades can chip or snap if heat treatment is uneven. *High.*
7. **Tariffs and the trade-data signal.** The IEEPA tariffs were struck down on 20 Feb 2026. A 10% Section 122 surcharge applied from 24 Feb to 24 Jul 2026 and was replaced by a 12.5% Section 301 forced-labor duty.\[48\]\[49\] Kitchen knives (8211.92) are on List 4A at 7.5%, while pocket and folding knives (8211.93) are on List 3 at 25%.\[14\]\[50\] The forced-labor duty is reportedly under legal challenge, and 178 Section 301 exclusions expire Nov 2026.\[51\]\[52\]\[53\] The OEC trailing data showing a fall in US-bound shipments may partly reflect stockpiling and reporting lags. *Medium.*
8. **Seller marketing.** Treat HRC ranges, "powder steel," "VG-10 equivalent" and Damascus layer counts on product pages as unverified marketing unless they are backed by independent testing. *High.*
