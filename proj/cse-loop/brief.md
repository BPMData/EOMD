## 1. The ask
A quick, looping, 30–60 second video about PLTW's **Computer Science Essentials (CSE)**, made to play on hallway TVs
in Danbury High School's **Information Technology & Cybersecurity Academy**. Bryan will also share it with CS
educators in an online class he's taking. Due: "let's just... make it right now."

## 2. Purpose and tone
**Viewer:** students walking the Academy's halls "who might be interested in computer science", including kids who
have never coded.
**Takeaway (Bryan's words):** "Oh, fun. If I take Computer Science Essentials I'll learn and do all kinds of neat
stuff!" Kids who have never coded "should be able to look at this and say, 'Oh, I could take that class and succeed
in it!'"
- **Know:** in CSE you make real things: apps, self-driving robot cars, Python programs, and (at Danbury) micro:bit
  gadgets and drones. No coding experience needed.
- **Feel:** fun, and "I could do that."
- **Do:** ask their counselor about the class (§10).

**Tone:** futuristic, fun, creative, innovative. "Edutainment utopianism 'bounce'."
**Must NOT feel:** demoscene, nerdy, anything with a high barrier to entry; "too dreary and heavy"; Jetsons or
anything silly.
**"Futuristic" is a look, not a theme:** "a fresh, bright, sleek look that feels current ... the video that would be
playing on the overhead terminals in the lounge of a futuristic sleek glass corpo building in Neo-New York if Project
Lead the Way was some advanced multi-national edtech neocorp with a glass NYC HQ skyscraper." So: glass, light,
clean confident type and motion; no "the future" slogans (T06 holds).

## 3. How it's watched
- **Screen:** "your normal like 65–75 inch 1080 to cheap 4K HDTVs you see everywhere nowadays, like the menus at
  restaurants." Landscape 16:9. Designed at 1920×1080; must stay crisp when a cheap 4K set upscales it.
- **Distance:** "The hallways are like, 12 feet wide tops, so they'll be viewing them from anywhere from like 12 to 2
  feet away ... the text doesn't have to be HUGE." Sized for 12 ft on a 65" set: body ≥ 32 px, headlines ≥ 64 px at
  1080p (Academies' 20-ft sizes scaled by distance, rounded up for a glance in passing).
- **Room:** "kind of loud halls, so the audio should be able to hold its own" but "not be integral to the piece (okay
  if it's drowned out or muted)." The picture tells the whole story.
- **Attention:** students walk past and catch a few seconds; every beat must make sense on its own.
- **Repeats:** loops all day; the same students see it many times a day.
- **Plays from:** the HTML player in a browser on the hallway TV. Educators get a link on Bryan's GitHub Pages.

## 4. How the subject works
From PLTW's course outline (`sources/CSE_CourseOutline_CS.pdf`, BR015.06.2026): a full-year course, "an excellent
entry point for new high school computer science (CS) learners." Students start with block-based coding in MIT App
Inventor (apps), then blocks and text side by side (self-driving vehicles, maps, image processing), then Python
(games, simulations), and finish with a project of their own ("an app, a website, or a physical computing device").

At Danbury (Bryan, round 5): CSE is "the 10th grade prereq for all pathways" of the Information Technology &
Cybersecurity Academy. The three pathways: Computer Programming & Gaming, Cybersecurity, Networking.

**Never say or imply:**
- That it's easy. ("It's NOT really easy so don't say that.")
- That you need prior experience. It "DOES NOT require prior computer or coding experience": the point to land.
- That every PLTW CSE class uses the micro:bit or drones. They are Bryan's additions ("don't tell PLTW lmao"), shown
  as Danbury Academy extras, in the Academy's branding, never in PLTW's (Bryan, round 5).
- Promises the sources don't make (jobs, college credit, AP); one pathway or class ranked above another.
- Otherwise: "I trust your judgement not to say anything stupid."
