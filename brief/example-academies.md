# Example brief: the Academies hallway film

The prompt that produced the Academies film (`academies-film/`), written by Bryan and sent as a single message in the
Claude web UI, with no project files or earlier context. It is the bar for a brief: nearly every section of
`TEMPLATE.md` is in it, in the client's own words, with the reasons attached ("this drives every design decision").
The academy facts come from school materials (`proj/academies/brand.md`). Kept word for word below.

---

Create a complete 45–60 second looping motion-design film for The Academies of Danbury High School (Danbury, CT), to play continuously, with sound, on hallway display screens. Use pure vanilla JavaScript (Node.js for tooling and rendering is fine). The style is contemporary motion graphics at award-winning portfolio quality (think Motionographer / Behance / Awwwards-level craft). Concept, script, design system, illustration, animation, music, and sound design are all yours. Quality matters more than speed. Work autonomously through completion; I will be away from my computer.

THE PURPOSE
Show 9th graders, other students, and visiting families what the Academies of Danbury High School are and how the seven career academies give each student a real path to pursue. Viewers should feel "there's a place for me here" and know their next step. Tone: confident, warm, inclusive, a little playful. No clichés about "the future," no stock-corporate energy, no ranking one academy above another.

HOW IT WILL BE WATCHED (this drives every design decision)
- A landscape TV in a busy school hallway, looping all day, with sound on.
- Viewers are walking past at 8–12 feet. Most will see only a few seconds. Almost nobody will start watching at the beginning.
- Hallways are loud, so the film must also make complete sense with the sound drowned out.
- Staff and students will hear it hundreds of times a day. It must not become grating on repeat.

HOW THE SYSTEM WORKS (this shapes the story)
- All 9th graders start in the Exploration Academy, where they learn about the academies and pathways.
- During 9th grade, students RANK their pathway choices for grades 10–12.
- Pathways are open to every student and assigned by lottery, regardless of grades. Students who don't rank thoughtfully still get placed — sometimes somewhere that doesn't fit them.
- So the film's message is: explore, then rank carefully. Never say or imply students simply "choose" or "pick" an academy, and never promise placement.

LOOP REQUIREMENTS
- The film loops forever with no visible or audible seam. The final frame must flow directly into the first, and the music must loop seamlessly too (same tempo, key, and energy at the loop point; no hard cut, no fade-to-silence gap).
- Design the ending to return to the opening state (e.g., the seven paths re-wind into the spiral, which then unwinds again).
- Every academy beat must make sense on its own to someone who just walked up. Never depend on the viewer having seen an earlier beat.
- The school name and the core message ("Explore. Rank carefully.") should be readable at least twice per loop, not only on the end card.
- Hold any end card or message card for at least 4 seconds.

HOW TO DISPLAY ACADEMIES VS. PATHWAYS
- Each academy gets one beat. The ACADEMY NAME is the hero: large, kinetic, emphasized, readable at 12 feet.
- The three PATHWAY names appear in the same beat as clearly subordinate supporting text — smaller, lighter weight, ideally attached to the three hexagons of that academy's cluster (see Visual Language). They should be readable to someone who stops for a moment, but are never the focal point of any shot.
- Do not give any individual pathway its own beat or hero moment.
- Budget roughly 5–6 seconds per academy beat so passersby can actually read it.
- Show both campuses as equals. A light visual cue for which campus an academy lives on is fine; don't make campus a major story element.

BRANDING
Primary: Danbury Blue #0033A0 — the anchor color for the whole film.
Academy colors: as listed above, one per academy. Exploration Academy teal marks the loop's start/return point.
Neutrals: Hat Gray #A2AAAD (large shapes only — never text, ~1.9:1 on white), White #FFFFFF (knockout text).

Contrast rules:
- Use dark text on yellow, never white.
- Audit teal before using it as a fill behind text.
- All text must meet WCAG AA contrast against its background. Hallway TVs are often in bright light, so err toward stronger contrast.

VISUAL LANGUAGE (from the school's official branding — recreate procedurally in the same spirit; do not trace or copy the badges)
- Each official academy badge is a colored circle containing a cluster of THREE HEXAGONS, one per pathway, each holding a simple line icon, with a small emblem above the circle. Use this as the structural backbone: academy = the circle, pathways = the three hexes.
- The school logo is a multicolor spiral with a hat at its center. It is the strongest candidate for the signature recurring element and a natural loop point.
- Nickname: the Hatters (Danbury is historically "Hat City"). The hat can recur as a motif.
- Suggested spine (optional; beat it if you have better): the spiral unwinds or branches into seven paths; each path resolves into one academy's colored circle and three hexes; then the paths wind back into the spiral and the loop begins again. "Pathways" is the school's own word, so making it literal fits.

VERIFIED VISUAL DETAILS YOU MAY USE (West campus, from press coverage of the building)
EMT and allied health labs · prosthetic-design project spaces · a greenhouse-style lab with a skylight · a renewable energy lab · a stock ticker display in the Emerging Technology & Entrepreneurship wing.
Do not invent specific facilities for other academies. Represent them through their subject matter instead.

LANGUAGE
The film is primarily in English, plain-language, and readable at a glance.
Add a few deliberate multilingual moments for the school's large Spanish- and Brazilian Portuguese–speaking community — but ONLY with words an English-only viewer would instantly understand: close cognates (Energy / Energía / Energia, Medicine / Medicina, Art / Arte, Technology / Tecnología / Tecnologia) or universally known greetings (Hola / Olá). Treat these as a visual flourish, e.g., a word morphing through all three languages. Use Brazilian Portuguese spelling, not European. Two to four such moments per loop is plenty.

CALL TO ACTION
The school name ("The Academies of Danbury High School") plus a next step consistent with the ranking process — explore the academies in 9th grade, rank carefully, talk to your counselor. Do not fabricate deadlines, URLs, QR codes, or contact details. If you cannot verify a URL, leave it out.

MUSIC AND SOUND FOR REPEATED HALLWAY PLAYBACK
- Original composition, synthesized in code. Warm, upbeat, and steady — something people can hear 200 times a day without resenting it.
- One musical idea or color per academy, but all within a single cohesive, seamlessly looping piece. No big drop, no long silence, no ending cadence that makes the loop point obvious.
- Sound design synced to motion (soft whooshes, ticks, light impacts), mixed well under the music. Avoid harsh high-frequency risers, piercing tones, alarm-like sounds, or anything that sounds like a school bell or fire alarm.
- Mastering: target around -16 LUFS integrated, true peak no higher than -1 dBTP, no sudden loudness jumps anywhere in the loop, including across the loop point.
- The visuals must still tell the full story if the audio is drowned out by hallway noise.

HARD LIMITS ON CLAIMS
- Do not invent statistics, awards, rankings, enrollment numbers, partnerships, or sports/club claims. If it isn't in this prompt or verified from an official source, leave it out.
- Keep a sources.md listing every fact used and where it came from ("provided in prompt from school materials" is an acceptable source for the academy list and colors).
- No real student photos, faces, names, or likenesses. Fully illustrated / abstract / typographic.

HARD TECHNICAL CONSTRAINTS
- Pure JavaScript only. No external images, no stock footage, no remote fonts, no CDNs, no text-to-speech or other API calls. Everything is drawn procedurally (Canvas 2D and/or SVG, custom easing, timeline engine). Fonts must be system fonts or embedded from properly licensed files you document.
- No narration.
- Music and SFX synthesized in code (Web Audio API for playback; offline render in Node for the exported file).
- Build a small deterministic timeline engine: seekable, frame-accurate, driven by time rather than requestAnimationFrame accumulation, so any frame renders reproducibly and the loop point can be verified exactly.

CREATIVE EXPECTATIONS
- One memorable signature visual idea that recurs, seven distinct academy beats that share a system yet each feel specific, and a loop point that feels like a breath, not a restart.
- Expressive motion: anticipation, overshoot, follow-through, staggered reveals, match cuts and shape-morph transitions (hexagons are ideal morph targets), camera-style moves (push, parallax, rotate), rhythm locked to the music. No slideshow logic, no repeated templates, no default easings.
- Considered typography: strong hierarchy (academy names clearly dominant over pathway names), kinetic type, a tight type system, sized for hallway distance. Avoid thin weights and small text — they disappear on a TV across a hallway.
- Avoid rapid flashing or strobing (keep well under 3 flashes per second) — it's a public display.

DELIVERABLES
1. PRIMARY: Finished MP4, 1920×1080 landscape, H.264 video with AAC audio, loop-ready. Also provide the same file with no audio track, in case a display needs it.
2. SECONDARY: a 1080×1920 vertical version, only if the layout adapts cleanly rather than being squeezed.
3. A single self-contained HTML player that loops automatically, starts playing on load, fills the screen, and hides all controls unless the mouse moves (for kiosk-style playback on a computer connected to a display).
4. Editable vanilla JS project: timeline engine, scene modules, synthesized audio, and developer playback controls (play/pause/scrub/restart, mute).
5. Script/storyboard, sources.md, audio stems (music, SFX, master), and a README explaining how to edit and re-render. Put all academy names, pathway names, colors, and on-screen copy in ONE config file so a name or color can be corrected without touching scene code.
6. A ZIP of the complete project.

RENDER PIPELINE
Use what's available: headless Chromium (Playwright/Puppeteer) for frame capture, offline audio rendering in Node, ffmpeg for encoding. If a tool is unavailable, choose a suitable alternative and report material limitations honestly.

QA: DO NOT SKIP
Inspect rendered frames and finished exports. Check:
- The loop seam: render the last second plus the first second back-to-back and confirm there is no visible jump or audible click, gap, or loudness change.
- Legibility at hallway distance: academy names readable when the frame is viewed at small size (simulate a 1920×1080 frame shown at roughly 25% scale).
- Every academy beat understandable in isolation.
- Contrast on every academy color, especially yellow and teal.
- Pathway text subordinate but legible on pause.
- Audio loudness, true peak, clipping, and that nothing sounds harsh on repeat.
- Transition smoothness, dropped or duplicated frames.
- Factual accuracy against sources.md.
Revise anything that falls short, then deliver a short honest report of what you verified, what remains unverified, and any known limitations.

This task produces creative assets only. It does not involve publishing or distributing the film.
